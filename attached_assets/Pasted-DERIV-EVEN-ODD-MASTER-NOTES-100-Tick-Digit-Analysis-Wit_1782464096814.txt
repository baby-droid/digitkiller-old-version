CHAPTER 4
Conceptual "Fair Value Gap" (Digit Imbalance)

Without candlesticks, you can define an imbalance as one or more digits that appear much less frequently than expected in your observation window.

Example

Expected

10%

Actual

Digit 7 = 5%

You might label this a digit imbalance. Keep in mind there is no established evidence that such gaps must "fill."

CHAPTER 7
Best Percentage Zones to Observe

Rather than treating these as signals, use them as filters to study market behavior.

Balanced

Even 48–52%

Odd 48–52%

Moderate imbalance

55–45%

Strong imbalance

60–40%

Extreme imbalance

65–35% or greater

The more extreme the imbalance, the more unusual the sample—but unusual samples still occur by chance.

CHAPTER 11
Situations to Avoid Trading

Many traders choose to avoid trading when:

The last 100 ticks are close to 50/50 and provide no clear observation.
Digit frequencies are changing rapidly from one update to the next.
You are increasing stake size to recover losses.
You are relying on a single streak as proof of the next outcome.
CHAPTER 12
Entry Framework for Testing

Instead of fixed "buy here" rules, define objective conditions for paper testing.

Example checklist:

Observe 100-tick distribution.
Record even vs. odd percentage.
Note the lowest-frequency digits.
Wait for your predefined setup.
Risk a fixed amount.
Record the outcome.
Repeat over a large sample (e.g., 500–1,000 trades) before concluding whether the idea has value.
CHAPTER 13
Suggested Observation Window

Many traders experiment with:

100-tick distribution as context.
Entering only after a stable observation period.
Holding for a small, predefined number of ticks.

The "best" number of ticks cannot be stated as a fact because there is no verified universal optimum.
